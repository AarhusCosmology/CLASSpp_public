# Precision of 2026-10-27: C_l every 50 multipoles, the dark energy fluid on rkdp45, accurate approximation triggers, finer Bessel sampling

**Status (2026-10-27):** shipped in one pull request. It replaces the 2026-10-22 retune (#473, closed
unmerged; `2026-10-22-precision-retune-design.md` keeps its measurements). It also carries the date presets that
#480 and #483 left to #473. Not done: quintic interpolation of the flat Bessel functions (measured below, too
slow in the transfer kernel as it stands). Evidence tags: [M] measured here, [C] stated by the code, [P] an
earlier note, not re-measured.

## Metric and reference

The three-code benchmark (`benchmarks/codecmp/`, untracked) [M]. The likelihoods are Planck PR4 CamSpec TTTEEE,
low-l TT, LoLLiPoP EE, ACT DR6 lensing and DESI DR2. The draws are 160 LCDM and 112 w0waCDM posterior draws,
all at `l_max_scalars` 3000, which is what cobaya passes. b is the cosmological bias: the largest shift, in
posterior sigma, of any combination of cosmological parameters. S is the spread of delta chi^2 over the draws.

The reference is new: `ref2` (codecmp `models.py`). It is `precision_precise.pre` plus Boltzmann hierarchies
to 50, `delta_l_max` 2500, accurate lensing and halofit to 150/Mpc. It is converged to 0.012/0.006/0.004/0.24
in cosmic-variance units (TT/EE/TE/phiphi, l <= 3000) against a tighter run. LCDM and w0wa were generated by
the same build (fd07bace, before #480's k-range rule). The previous reference is 0.030 (LCDM) and 0.045 (w0wa)
in b from `ref2`. Measured against `ref2`, the 2026-10-26 loosenings of #473 cost more than #473 said: master
0.077, #473 rebased 0.165.

The spectrum accuracy is the median over draws, l = 30-2500, against `ref2`: the largest relative error, and
the significance sqrt(sum (2l+1)/2 (dC/C)^2) a cosmic-variance-limited measurement would see (TE against
TT EE + TE^2) (`ee_acc.py`).

## Where the bias comes from [M]

With accurate triggers, nearly all of the bias is in omega_b: -0.140 sigma at 45 / 2e-3, against -0.020 on
master. A screen raised one group of settings at a time to `ref2`'s values, on top of 45 / 2e-3 (96 LCDM
draws, `benchmarks/linstep50/score_wb*.out`, `plots/omega_b_screen.png`). No group removes the shift.

Four discretizations in the transfer stage each take 0.021-0.027 off it, all in the same direction:

* the Bessel table (`hyper_sampling_flat` 16);
* `l_logstep` 1.026 (`l_linstep` adds nothing to that);
* the transfer cuts (off);
* the source sampling in time (0.03).

The streaming trigger's second condition (0.013) and the lensing settings (0.011) add less. Tight coupling,
the ur fluid, ncdm, the Boltzmann hierarchies, the thermodynamics and background steps and halofit's k range
add nothing.

The effects add almost linearly. `l_logstep` 1.026 + `hyper_sampling_flat` 12 + cuts x1.3 brings omega_b to
-0.070 and b to 0.082, but costs +55%. In the spectra, the shift is a TT dip of -3 to -7e-4 across
l = 400-1200 (2nd-4th peaks). The cheap triggers' own error at l = 400-1200 had the opposite sign.

Price per change, one thread, warm cache:

| change on top of 45 / 2e-3 | b (96 draws) | time |
|---|---|---|
| `hyper_sampling_flat` 10 / 11 / 12 | 0.143 / 0.140 / 0.137 | +1.7% / +2.5% / +1.6-4.0% |
| transfer cuts x1.15 / x1.3 | 0.150 / 0.143 | +2.6-5.4% / +5.2-8.4% |
| `hyper_sampling_flat` 12 + cuts x1.15 | 0.119 | +3-7% |
| `l_logstep` 1.06 / 1.026 | 0.149 / 0.138 | +10% / +32% |
| `perturb_sampling_stepsize` 0.08 / 0.06 | 0.157 / 0.152 | (not timed; 0.05 was +43% on 2026-10-22) |

**Why the Bessel table.** The flat-space Bessel functions are interpolated by cubic Hermite polynomials
between tabulated values and derivatives [C]. At 8 points per period (">7.5" is the documented minimum) that
leaves a few 1e-4 in the transfer functions.

The proof is the interpolation order. Quintic Hermite also uses the second derivative, which the Bessel
equation gives from the value and the first derivative. It was already implemented for curved space at low nu
[C]. In flat space it gives b 0.130 at 8 and at 6 points per period alike, as converged as the cubic at 16
(0.132).

A fast quintic path, located once per wavenumber like #476's cubic, agrees with the generic one to 1e-10. It
costs +10% on one thread, though, because the kernel is memory-bound and reads a third table array and two
more weights per point. The cubic at 12 gets 80% of the gain for 1-4%.

Folding the second derivative into the per-point weights through the Bessel equation would make the quintic
read 11 values per point instead of 13 (the cubic reads 9). That is a follow-up, worth it if it lands near the
cubic's cost: the table could then shrink to 6 points per period.

## Not taken

* **`tight_coupling_trigger_tau_c_over_tau_k` 0.01:** removes the remaining EE bump at l = 100-200 (+9.6e-4),
  but moves n_s (b 0.173).
* **The ur fluid trigger at 130 and accurate lensing:** each halves the +4-6e-4 in TT and EE at l > 1800, but
  does not move the posterior.
* **Transfer cuts x1.15 on top (option C):** b 0.119 for another 2-6%.
* **`l_logstep` and the source sampling:** 10-40% per 0.02 sigma.

## The fluid on rkdp45 [M]

`benchmarks/fluid_rkdp45/fluid_rk.py` reconstructs the 2026-10-22 check and gives its numbers again to the
digit. It runs ndf15 and rkdp45 at the default tolerance against ndf15 at 1e-5, on nine fluids:

* CPL w0wa with and without PPF;
* constant w with cs2 = 1, 0.01 and 1e-6;
* PEDE and GPEDE;
* the pheno-axion fluid at n = 2 and 3;
* w0wa in the newtonian gauge.

rkdp45 is as close to the converged run as ndf15 (C_l <= 7e-6 of their peak, P(k) <= 8e-5) and 35-60% faster.
On the w0wa draws the evolver moves b by less than its error (0.137 -> 0.141 at step 40, 0.139 -> 0.144 at
step 50, +-0.006). One thread goes from 1.222 s to 0.783 s.

## Validation [M]

ctest 62/62; `test-evolver-selection` asserts the fluid's choice. TEST_LEVEL=2 against master's classyref:
228 of 1512 scenarios differ, all of them CMB C_l (every scenario with tensors, and 48 of the 144 scalar
ones with polarization).

**The scalar ones.** They were adjudicated against converged runs of today's code on eight models
(`codecmp/adjudicate_cpp/run2.py`, `compare2.py`, output `out4/`). The models are LCDM; 3 x 0.06, 1.5 x 0.03
and 1 x 0.3 eV neutrinos; Omega_k = +-0.01; a scalar field; and w0wa. The reference is `precision_precise.pre`
with `k_step_super` 2e-3, accurate lensing and `delta_l_max` 1800. On all eight, every raw and lensed TT, EE
and TE spectrum is closer to it than master's, in cosmic-variance significance over l = 2-3000:

* lensed EE 2.28-2.70 -> 0.88-1.40;
* lensed TE 1.13-1.59 -> 0.53-1.01;
* lensed TT 0.92-1.24 -> 0.87-1.08.

C_L^phiphi moves by -0.10 to +0.08 and lensed BB by +1.5%, both at about 17 CV for both codes. P(k) is
unchanged except w0wa's linear P(k), whose error goes from 3.5e-3 to 3.9e-3 (k <= 1/Mpc). That is ndf15 at
its default tolerance landing 4e-4 closer by chance: ndf15 at 1e-5 gives 3.8e-3, as rkdp45 does at any
tolerance, and LCDM shows the same 3.9e-3.

**The tensor ones.** At step 50 alone, the 40 differing scenarios all had tensors. Against a run converged in l (step 5), the
tensor-only ones are closer than master (largest error 2.3e-2 against 8.6e-2, at l_max_tensors = 500). The
scalar-field ones differ only at the tensor cut-off in lensed BB (l = 505, both 32-40% off converged)
(`benchmarks/linstep50/tensor_adj.py`, `scf_adj.py`).

## Date presets

`pre/precision_pre_2026_10_27.pre` restores `l_linstep` 40, `delta_l_max` 500, the triggers 26 / 0.008 and
`hyper_sampling_flat` 8. On one thread it is 8% slower than the new defaults, and 61% slower on w0wa. The
older date presets pin the same values.

Two things have no fixed key:

* **The old k range.** For lensed C_l pass `k_max_tau0_above_l_max` = 0.8 `l_max_scalars` + 900, without
  lensing 0.
* **The fluid's evolver.** Pass `evolver_perturbations` = 1.

`act_dr6.pre` pins `hyper_sampling_flat` 8. It and `precision_precise.pre` set their own steps, triggers and
headroom.
