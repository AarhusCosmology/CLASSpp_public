# HMcode 2020

**Status (2026-10-09).** Implemented on branch `hmcode-2020`. HMcode 2020, with
and without its baryonic feedback, is the default HMcode. HMcode 2016 is
bit-identical in flat cosmologies without a fluid; its dark-energy correction
now follows HMcode, which takes w0wa and curved cosmologies from 1.6e-2 and
1.4e-2 to 1.2e-3 against CAMB (§HMcode 2016: the Dolag correction). Against CAMB
2.0.4, which runs Mead's own HMcode, both versions agree to 1.2e-3 for
k ≤ 5/Mpc in ΛCDM, w0wa, curved and feedback cosmologies, and to 4.4e-3 with
massive neutrinos. That excess is CLASS++'s linear spectrum under the
neutrino defaults of #397, not HMcode (§Massive neutrinos). The branch also
fixes the sign of the curvature correction in the spline integrals of
`tools/arrays.cpp` (§The spline integrals). Seven differences from class_public
were found on the way and reported as lesgourg/class#269 (§Differences from
class_public).

Evidence tags: **[M]** measured here, **[C]** the code says so, **[P]** from
the paper, **[?]** no evidence.

## Problem

CLASS++ implements HMcode 2016 only (Mead et al. 2016, arXiv:1602.02154).
Current analyses use HMcode 2020 (Mead et al. 2021, arXiv:2009.01858), often
with its one-parameter baryonic feedback model: Cobaya setups, ACT and DES
lensing, cosmic shear. In CLASS++, `hmcode_version` is not read, so classy
refuses it as an unread parameter. PR #448 listed this as outstanding.

class_public `devel` has had HMcode 2020 since #208 (Feb 2025, Nils Schöneberg
and Julien Lesgourgues), in `external/HMcode/hmcode.c`. There, 2020 is the
default `hmcode_version`. **[C]**

### Measured starting point [M]

ΛCDM with massless neutrinos, nonlinear boost P_nl/P_lin, k ≤ 5/Mpc:

* CLASS++'s HMcode 2016 against CAMB 2.0.4 `mead2016`: ≤ 5e-4 at z = 0, 1, 2.
  CAMB `mead2016` is Mead's own Fortran.
* CAMB `mead2020` against `mead2016`: up to 6% at z = 0, 2–3% at z = 1–2. The
  version matters at the percent level.

## Decisions

* **Default.** `non_linear = hmcode` without `hmcode_version` means 2020, as in
  class_public. The 2016 model remains available as `hmcode_version = 2016`
  (or `2015`), and `pre/precision_pre_2026_08_21.pre` sets it, to restore the
  previous behaviour.
* **Versions.** `2016`/`2015`/`16`/`15`, `2020`/`20`, and
  `2020_baryonic_feedback`, with `log10T_heat_hmcode` (default 7.8), as in
  class_public. They are matched exactly: class_public searches for "20" in
  the string, so it reads `2018` as 2020. The 2016 feedback inputs
  (`feedback model`, `eta_0`, `c_min`) apply to 2016 only, and are refused with
  2020. `log10T_heat_hmcode` without feedback, and `nk_wiggle` with 2016, are
  refused too.
* **Location.** HMcode stays in `source/nonlinear_module.cpp` (the user's
  choice); it is not split into its own file.
* **Reference.** CAMB's HMcode where it and class_public differ.
* **2016's dark-energy correction follows HMcode** (the user's choice,
  2026-10-09), although that changes 2016 output with a fluid or curvature.
* **The neutrino defaults are not changed here.** The cause of the m_ν excess
  is outside HMcode, and those defaults were tuned on the Planck S of #397
  (§Massive neutrinos).

## Design

The port follows class_public's physics and restructures it, in the same
spirit as the transfer restructure
(`2026-10-08-transfer-restructure-design.md`).

**The version-dependent parameters in one place.** class_public's
`hmcode_compute()` has six `switch (version)` blocks scattered through one
function. `nonlinear_hmcode_compute()` sets, in one block each:

* the collapse threshold δ_c and the virial overdensity Δ_v;
* the concentration amplitude A, the transition exponent α, the halo bloating
  η, the 1-halo damping k* and the 2-halo damping f_damp and k_damp;
* the halo window: the factor (1 − f_ν) for 2020, and the gas and star
  fractions for the feedback model.

The mass-function integral, the concentration-mass relation and the
combination [P_1h^α + P_2h^α]^(1/α) are written once, for all versions. The
constants are those of table 2 of the paper, as in CAMB; class_public uses
longer forms of the same fits. **[C]**

**The version is an argument, not state.** class_public computes the feedback
model as a ratio of three runs (feedback × fitted / unfitted), and switches
between them by overwriting `pfo->hm_version`. Here each run of
`nonlinear_hmcode_compute()` receives its version explicitly, and
`nonlinear_hmcode()` combines them.

**New ingredients, needed by 2020 only:**

* **Growth without radiation.** HMcode integrates its own linear growth in a
  cosmology without radiation: g(a), its accumulated integral and Ω_m(a), on
  128 points in a from 1e-4 to 1, at a fixed tolerance of 1e-10 (ndf15). This
  keeps P_nl/P_lin identical to the original code. The inputs are Ω_m, Ω_b, the
  dark-energy densities, the radiation-like neutrino density from N_eff, and
  the fluid's own w(a) and ρ(a) through `background_w_fld()`. HMcode knows the
  fluid as w0 + (1 − a) wa, which this reproduces for that parametrisation;
  class_public and CAMB pass w0 and wa only, which for PEDE would be its
  linearisation at a = 1 (3.6e-4 / 1.1e-3 in the boost) [M]. Curvature is kept
  in H², as in CAMB.
* **What it refuses.** Dark energy other than Λ and the fluid, and fields that
  do not cluster, beyond 1e-4 of today's density. The second needs a species
  query, `NonClusteringOmega0()`: the background counts an `EnergyType::Other`
  species as effective matter ρ − 3p plus effective radiation 3p, so a
  quintessence-like scalar field never shows in Ω_de. class_public refuses
  scalar fields only. Components that have diluted away by today (NEDE, an
  early modification of gravity) are not refused: the growth of HMcode omits
  the early universe by design, radiation included, and Mead's code in CAMB
  sees any dark energy other than a w0-wa fluid as w = −1 with today's
  density [C]. Refusing them by presence would make every NEDE or EMG run with
  `non_linear = hmcode` fail.
* **De-wiggling.** The BAO wiggles of the 2-halo term are damped by
  exp(−k² σ_v²). The no-wiggle spectrum is the linear one divided by the
  Eisenstein & Hu no-wiggle fit, smoothed with a Gaussian in ln k
  (σ = 0.25, 5e-3 to 5 h/Mpc, `nk_wiggle` points, default 512), then
  multiplied back. The Gaussian weights are computed once. The split is redone
  at each time from the linear spectrum; CAMB extracts it once at z = 0 and
  scales it with the growth unless f_ν is large. Only the fitted 2020 model
  de-wiggles; the unfitted and feedback variants use the linear spectrum in
  the 2-halo term, as in CAMB. **[C]**
* **Concentration.** The formation redshift comes from inverting the
  normalised growth table.
* **Dark-energy correction (Dolag et al. 2004)**, in every cosmology, as in
  CAMB: g(z_∞)/g_ΛCDM(z_∞) × g_ΛCDM(z)/g(z), with g the growth without radiation
  and g_ΛCDM that of a flat ΛCDM with the same Ω_m. **[C]**

Halofit, the spectra and lensing modules, and the classy interface are
unchanged, apart from the new input keys.

## Alternatives rejected

* **Line-by-line port of `hmcode.c`.** Easiest to diff against upstream, but it
  adds about 1500 lines of switch-heavy code to a 3700-line file, which is the
  structure Julien's transfer audit argued against.
* **Port from Mead's Fortran in CAMB.** Authoritative, but its structure differs
  from the existing CLASS++ HMcode, and class_public already follows it. CAMB
  serves as the reference instead.
* **CLASS's own growth factor instead of HMcode's radiation-free one.** Simpler,
  but it changes P_nl/P_lin away from the original code by an amount Mead's
  calibration did not see.

## Results

### Against CAMB 2.0.4 [M]

Maximum of |boost_CLASS++/boost_CAMB − 1| over z = 0, 0.5, 1, 2, for k ≤ 1 and
k ≤ 5/Mpc. The CLASS++ runs use `P_k_max_1/Mpc = 20`. Harness:
`benchmarks/hmcode/` (untracked).

| cosmology | 2020 | 2016 |
|---|---|---|
| ΛCDM | 1.1e-3 / 1.1e-3 | 6.8e-4 / 6.8e-4 |
| m_ν = 0.06 eV | 1.7e-3 / 3.3e-3 | 1.8e-3 / 4.4e-3 |
| m_ν = 0.3 eV | 1.0e-3 / 2.9e-3 | 1.0e-3 / 5.1e-3 |
| w0 = −0.9, wa = 0.1 (PPF in CAMB) | 9.9e-4 / 9.9e-4 | 4.6e-4 / 4.6e-4 |
| Ω_k = 0.02 | 8.9e-4 / 9.4e-4 | 8.3e-4 / 1.2e-3 |
| feedback, log10 T = 7.3, 7.8, 8.3 | 1.1e-3 / 1.1e-3 | — |
| feedback 7.8, m_ν = 0.06 eV | 1.7e-3 / 3.4e-3 | — |

* The remaining ~1e-3 has the same shape in both versions: below 1e-4 for
  k ≤ 0.05/Mpc, then about −5e-4 to −8e-4 at z = 0, decreasing with z. It is
  common to the two halo models, so it comes from their shared inputs (the
  linear spectra of the two codes), not from the port; class devel's own
  HMcode 2020 measures 9.7e-4 in ΛCDM. The 2020-specific part, in the BAO range
  k = 0.08–0.2/Mpc, is below 5e-4. The m_ν rows are larger for another reason
  (§Massive neutrinos).
* σ8 differs by 3e-4 between the codes (CAMB 0.82276, CLASS++ 0.82302).
* The unit test `TestHMcode` in `python/test_class.py` pins the 2016, 2020 and
  feedback boosts to CAMB values at k = 0.1, 0.3, 1 and 3/Mpc, z = 0 and 1, to
  3e-3, and both versions for w0wa and Ω_k = 0.02. The versions differ by 3–6%
  at three of those k.

### HMcode 2016: the Dolag correction [C, M]

Before this branch, CLASS++ 2016 was 1.6e-2 off CAMB for w0 = −0.9, wa = 0.1
and 1.4e-2 for Ω_k = 0.02. Both come from the dark-energy correction of the
concentrations (Dolag et al. 2004), (g(z_∞)/g_ΛCDM(z_∞))^1.5:

* With a fluid, it was applied only to haloes whose formation redshift exceeds
  z, so it never acted at z = 0 and the gap grew with z. HMcode, and class
  devel since #208, apply it to every halo below z_∞. Doing so: 4.6e-4.
* Without a fluid it was 1, and its ΛCDM reference had the actual curvature.
  HMcode computes it in every cosmology, against a flat ΛCDM with the same Ω_m.
  With both changes, Ω_k = 0.02: 1.2e-3.

Flat cosmologies without a fluid, with or without massive neutrinos, are
bit-identical, and so is all of 2020. Class devel's 2016 still has the
curvature part (1.7e-2).

### Massive neutrinos [M]

The m_ν rows exceed ΛCDM in both versions, by the same amount. class devel's
own HMcode measures 1.1e-3 for m_ν = 0.06 eV (2016: 5.3e-4), so the excess is
specific to CLASS++, and it is in the linear spectrum:

* Relative to CAMB and normalised at k = 0.01/Mpc, CLASS++'s linear P_m at z = 0
  falls with k: −1.1e-3 at k = 1, −3.8e-3 at k = 5/Mpc (class devel −1.2e-3).
  The boost rises by the same amount, since the 1-halo term depends on σ(R),
  not on P_lin at that k. HMcode's parameters in the two CLASS codes agree to
  1e-3 (σ8_cb 6e-4, r_nl 1e-3).
* The cause is three neutrino defaults of #397: `ncdm_fluid_trigger_tau_over_tau_k
  = 15`, `l_max_ncdm = 10`, `tol_ncdm_synchronous = 0.03`. Against a converged
  run (trigger 200, `l_max_ncdm` 120, tolerance 1e-5; trigger 50 and l_max 30
  agree with it to 6e-5), the defaults are −1.3e-3 at k = 1 and −3.8e-3 at
  k = 5. The error is the same for m_ν = 0.06 and 0.3 eV: at kτ = 15 the
  neutrinos are still relativistic at those k.
* The pre-#397 values (31, 17, 0.0034) are within 4.3e-4 of converged. With
  them, HMcode against CAMB for m_ν = 0.06 eV is 1.1e-3 (2020), 7.4e-4 (2016)
  and 1.1e-3 (feedback 7.8). m_ν = 0.3 eV stays at 2.8e-3 for k ≤ 5; class
  devel has 3.7e-3 there, and the cause is not known [?].
* Cost, Planck-like lensed run with P(k) and m_ν = 0.06 eV: 1.56 → 1.60 s on
  one thread (+3%), no measurable change on 11.
* CMB, at the ACT-like point of #448: the neutrino settings alone are 0.051 in
  ACT-lite distance at the defaults and 0.002 with the pre-#397 values. TT and
  EE move by 8e-4 below ℓ = 2000 (2.5e-4 with the old values), C_ℓ^φφ by
  1.8e-3 (1e-3).
* Two traps on the way. Without the fluid approximation the hierarchy is not a
  reference: truncated at `l_max_ncdm = 50` it sits 1e-2 above CAMB at k = 5.
  And class's mPk output files, at the default 10 points per decade, cost up to
  4e-3 in a boost interpolated near k = 1; class devel was measured at 100.

The defaults are not changed here: they were tuned on the Planck S of #397,
which this branch does not re-measure. They went back to the pre-#397 values in a
separate change (2026-10-09), see
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-ncdm-precision-defaults-design.md`.

### The spline integrals [M]

`array_integrate_spline_table_line_to_line`, `array_integrate_all_spline` and
`array_integrate_all_trapzd_or_spline` added the curvature correction of the
cubic spline to the trapezoid, instead of subtracting it. That doubled the
trapezoidal error instead of removing it (unit test: 8.3e-3 against the
trapezoid's 4.1e-3; 3.2e-5 after the fix). class_public fixed the same sign
after v3.2 (credits: C. Radermacher). The callers are the C_ℓ k-integral of the
spectra module, six integrals in thermodynamics, and σ, Halofit and HMcode.

Against a reference with a much finer q sampling (`q_linstep = 0.08`,
`q_logstep_spline = 2000`), as max |ΔD_ℓ| / max D_ℓ (normalised to the peak):

| | TT | EE | φφ | lensed BB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sign + | 3.1e-4 | 4.8e-5 | 1.2e-4 | 2.9e-3 |
| sign − | 4.9e-5 | 6.9e-7 | 8.7e-7 | 2.2e-8 |

Pointwise, the error sits at low ℓ:

| | TT | EE | φφ |
|---|---|---|---|
| sign + | 1.5e-3 (ℓ = 2) | 2.4e-3 (ℓ = 2) | 3.8e-3 (L = 46) |
| sign − | 3.0e-4 (ℓ = 19) | 4.7e-4 (ℓ = 38) | 5.3e-6 (L = 28) |

This is what `test_class.py` level 2 sees against a master `classyref`: φφ
fails its 3e-3 tolerance in 288 scenarios, 3.75e-3 at L = 46. The fast-math
floor between the two builds is 4e-6, measured with master built both ways
[M].

σ8 moves by 6e-5, the nonlinear P(k) by 2–4e-4, τ_reio by 2e-6, r_s,drag by
2e-6. The fix has no input key. The Planck S of the defaults was not
re-measured.

### Differences from class_public [C, M]

Found while porting; each is resolved here as CAMB does it. Reported as
lesgourg/class#269, with class devel measured against CAMB: feedback at
log10 T = 8.3 is 1.0e-2 off, Ω_k = 0.02 is 2.6e-2 off in 2020 and 1.7e-2 in 2016.

1. **k\* is zero in the unfitted and feedback variants.** The 1-halo damping
   then never acts, and the feedback response is wrong by 1e-2 at
   log10 T = 8.3 [M].
2. **The curvature is dropped from the growth without radiation**
   (Ω_m := 1 − Ω_Λ − Ω_fld): 1.2e-2 / 3.9e-2 against CAMB at Ω_k = 0.02 [M].
3. **The Dolag correction is applied only with a fluid**; HMcode applies it in
   every cosmology, since the two growth functions are different
   approximations.
4. **The ΛCDM reference of that correction** must be flat with the same Ω_m;
   the growth helper of class_public, and of CLASS++ 2016, uses the actual Ω_k
   and Ω_de. That accounts for most of the
   curved-case difference above: with curvature in H² alone the gap was
   2.6e-3 / 1.1e-2 [M].
5. **The wiggle grid** runs from 5e-3 to 5 in 1/Mpc instead of h/Mpc.
6. **The edge of the Gaussian smoothing** is tested with C's integer `abs()` on
   a distance in ln k, which truncates it: the edge is effectively 1 instead
   of 3σ = 0.75.
7. **Version strings** are matched by substring, so `2018` reads as 2020.

## Verification

* Reference CAMB 2.0.4, as tabulated above. Massive neutrinos are the worst
  case, at 3.4e-3 (2016: 4.4e-3), from the linear spectrum (§Massive neutrinos).
* 2016 against the branch before the port: ≤ 6e-13 in every case [M]. After
  the Dolag fix, bit-identical in flat cosmologies without a fluid [M].
* Errors: unknown versions, the inputs of one version given to another, and a
  scalar field with 2020 are refused with explicit messages (`TestHMcode`).
* AddressSanitizer: 2020, feedback with m_ν, w0wa with `nk_wiggle = 16`, 2016
  with m_ν = 0.3 eV and Ω_k = 0.02, with lensed C_ℓ: clean.
* `ctest`: 49/49. `python/test_class.py -m "not test_scenario"`: 132 passed.
* `python/test_class.py` level 2 against master: 288 φφ failures, all from the
  spline fix (§The spline integrals).
